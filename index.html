How the numbers are worked out
  • Each half-hour of your usage is multiplied by the tariff's unit rate for that half-hour. Standing charges are added for every day in your data. Yearly figures are scaled from the days you have.
  • Today's rates applies each tariff's current prices by time of day to all of your history, which is the fairest way to choose what to switch to. Rates at the time uses the price that was in force on each date, where Octopus has it.
  • EV charging is any half-hour over the detection threshold, minus your normal household use for that time of day. If you shift moves that charging into the cheapest half-hours of each tariff between plug-in and departure, at no more than your charger's power. It also moves the set share of your other usage into each day's cheapest slots. It never makes a day cost more than as used.
  • Intelligent Octopus Go also bills extra smart-charging slots outside 23:30–05:30 at the cheap rate. Octopus's consumption data doesn't show which slots those were, so the "as used" figure for Intelligent Go can be slightly high.
  • Gas is priced the same way, per half-hour or per day depending on what your meter sends. There's no shifting for gas. Readings in m³ are converted to kWh as m³ × 1.02264 × calorific value ÷ 3.6, the formula suppliers use on bills.
  • Your yearly bill adds the cheapest electricity tariff you can get (if you shift) to the cheapest gas tariff. Tariffs that need equipment you haven't ticked are left out.
  • Octopus, EDF, E.ON Next and Sainsbury's Energy publish their tariffs through public APIs, so their prices for your region are fetched live. Only the newest version of each tariff that's on sale today is shown. E.ON's EV and heat pump gas tariffs only come with their matching electricity tariff, and the best-bill figure respects that.
  • For tariffs where the cheap rate applies to car charging only (such as Scottish Power EV Optimise or OVO Charge Anytime), detected EV charging is billed at the car rate whenever it happens, and everything else at the standard rate. These suppliers usually only discount charging that their app schedules, often with a monthly cap, so treat these figures as a best case.
  • Export payments aren't included yet.
